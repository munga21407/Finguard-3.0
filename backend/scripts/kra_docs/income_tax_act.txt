KENYA REVENUE AUTHORITY — INCOME TAX ACT (CAP 470)
Consolidated KRA Guidance for Corporate Income Tax and SME Taxation | Updated 2024

SECTION 1: CORPORATE INCOME TAX (CIT) RATE
--------------------------------------------
The standard rate of Corporate Income Tax (CIT) applicable to resident companies
in Kenya is 30 percent (30%) on net taxable income.

Net taxable income is calculated as:
  Gross Revenue − Allowable Business Deductions = Net Profit
  Net Profit × 30% = Corporate Income Tax Due

For non-resident companies, the applicable rate is 37.5% on taxable income derived
from or accruing in Kenya.

Companies listed on the Nairobi Securities Exchange (NSE) enjoy a reduced rate of
25% for the first 5 years following listing, provided at least 25% of shares are
offered to the public.

SECTION 2: SMALL BUSINESS TURNOVER TAX (TOT)
----------------------------------------------
Small businesses with gross annual turnover between KES 1,000,000 and KES 50,000,000
may opt to be taxed under the Turnover Tax (TOT) regime instead of the standard
Corporate Income Tax regime.

Turnover Tax rate: 3% of gross monthly turnover.
TOT filing and payment deadline: 20th day of the following month.

Businesses with turnover below KES 1,000,000 are not required to pay income tax but
must file a nil return.

Businesses with turnover above KES 50,000,000 must use the standard CIT regime at 30%.

SECTION 3: ALLOWABLE DEDUCTIONS
---------------------------------
The following expenditures are deductible against gross income when computing taxable
profit for CIT purposes:

3.1 Revenue Expenditure
  - Salaries, wages, and benefits paid to employees (PAYE compliant)
  - Rent and premises costs for business use
  - Utility bills (electricity, water, internet, telephone) for business use
  - Professional services fees (legal, audit, consultancy)
  - Marketing and advertising expenses
  - Office supplies and consumables
  - Bank charges and financial service fees

3.2 Capital Allowances
  - Wear and tear allowance on plant and machinery: 12.5% per annum (reducing balance)
  - Industrial building allowance: 10% of cost per annum
  - Computer and digital equipment: 30% per annum (reducing balance)
  - Motor vehicles: 25% per annum (reducing balance)

3.3 Non-Deductible Expenses
  - Personal expenditure of owners or directors
  - Income tax payments (CIT, instalment tax)
  - Fines and penalties paid to government authorities
  - Entertainment expenses exceeding 1% of gross income
  - Depreciation (replaced by KRA capital allowances)

SECTION 4: INSTALMENT TAX PAYMENTS
-------------------------------------
Companies with an estimated annual tax liability exceeding KES 40,000 must pay
instalment tax in four equal instalments:

  1st Instalment: 25% — by 20th of the 4th month of the accounting year
  2nd Instalment: 25% — by 20th of the 6th month
  3rd Instalment: 25% — by 20th of the 9th month
  4th Instalment: 25% — by 20th of the 12th month

Penalty for underpayment of instalment tax: 20% of the difference between the
instalment paid and the actual tax due.

SECTION 5: ANNUAL TAX RETURN FILING
--------------------------------------
Companies must file their annual income tax return (IT2C form) within 6 months after
the end of their accounting year. For companies with December year-end, the deadline
is 30 June of the following year.

Late filing penalty: 5% of tax due per month, capped at 25% of total tax due.

SECTION 6: PAY AS YOU EARN (PAYE)
-----------------------------------
Employers are required to deduct PAYE from employee salaries and remit to KRA by the
9th day of the following month.

PAYE Tax Bands (2024):
  KES 0 – 24,000/month: 10%
  KES 24,001 – 32,333/month: 25%
  KES 32,334 and above: 30%

Personal Relief: KES 2,400 per month (KES 28,800 per annum)
Insurance Relief: 15% of premiums paid, max KES 5,000 per month

SECTION 7: WITHHOLDING TAX
----------------------------
Withholding Tax (WHT) is deducted at source by the payer on specific payments:

  - Dividends to resident shareholders: 5%
  - Dividends to non-resident shareholders: 15%
  - Interest paid to residents: 15%
  - Management and professional fees to residents: 5%
  - Royalties to residents: 5%
  - Royalties to non-residents: 20%
  - Construction contracts (to residents): 3%
  - Rental income (gross rent): 7.5% (resident landlords)

WHT must be remitted to KRA by the 20th of the month following the deduction.

SECTION 8: TRANSFER PRICING RULES
------------------------------------
Companies entering into transactions with related parties (defined as holding 25%+
equity interest) must ensure pricing conforms to the arm's length principle.

Documentation required:
  - Transfer Pricing Policy document
  - Comparability analysis
  - Selection and application of the most appropriate method

Penalty for non-compliance: KES 1,000,000 plus 2% of the value of cross-border
transactions without contemporaneous documentation.

SECTION 9: ANTI-AVOIDANCE PROVISIONS
---------------------------------------
KRA has broad powers to disregard transactions that lack commercial substance and
are entered into primarily to reduce tax liability. The Commissioner may:
  - Recharacterize transactions to reflect their economic substance
  - Assess tax based on the reconstructed transaction
  - Apply the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR)

SECTION 10: RECORD KEEPING
----------------------------
All companies must retain financial records for a minimum of 5 years, including:
  - Audited financial statements
  - Supporting schedules (trial balance, depreciation, debtors/creditors)
  - Payroll records and PAYE remittance receipts
  - Bank statements and reconciliations
  - Board minutes approving financial transactions
  - Contracts and agreements with related parties
