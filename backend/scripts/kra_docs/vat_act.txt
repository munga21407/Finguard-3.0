KENYA REVENUE AUTHORITY — VALUE ADDED TAX ACT (CAP 476)
Consolidated KRA Guidance for SME Compliance | Updated 2024

SECTION 1: VAT REGISTRATION THRESHOLD
---------------------------------------
An enterprise whose cumulative taxable supplies exceed KES 5,000,000 in any twelve-month
period is required to register for Value Added Tax (VAT) with the Kenya Revenue Authority.
Registration must be completed within 30 days of exceeding the threshold. Voluntary
registration is permitted for businesses with annual turnover below the threshold.

Penalty for failure to register: KES 10,000 or 5% of the tax due, whichever is higher.

SECTION 2: STANDARD VAT RATE
------------------------------
The standard rate of Value Added Tax applicable to taxable supplies of goods and services
made in Kenya is 16 percent (16%). This rate applies to all supplies not specifically
classified as zero-rated or exempt under the Third Schedule of the VAT Act.

From 1 January 2024, digital marketplace supplies are subject to the standard 16% VAT rate.

SECTION 3: ZERO-RATED SUPPLIES
--------------------------------
The following supplies are zero-rated (0% VAT, but VAT registration is still required):
- Exports of goods and services to consumers outside Kenya
- Supply of maize flour, cassava flour, wheat or meslin flour packaged in retail form
- Supply of milk and cream, unprocessed, raw or unconcentrated
- Supply of bread (excluding pastries, cakes, and biscuits)
- Inputs for the manufacture of medicaments
- Inputs for the manufacture of agricultural equipment
- Services supplied to non-resident persons for use outside Kenya
- Transportation of goods by air or sea to a place outside Kenya

SECTION 4: EXEMPT SUPPLIES
----------------------------
The following supplies are exempt from VAT (no VAT charged and no input VAT recovery):
- Financial services (banking, insurance, lending)
- Educational services provided by licensed institutions
- Medical and veterinary services
- Supply of residential premises for occupation as a private dwelling
- Postal services under the Postal Corporation of Kenya Act
- Unprocessed agricultural produce (primary sector)
- Public transportation (buses licensed for 25+ passengers)

SECTION 5: VAT FILING AND PAYMENT DEADLINES
---------------------------------------------
VAT returns must be filed and payment made by the 20th day of the month following the
taxable period. The taxable period is one calendar month.

Late filing penalty: KES 10,000 or 5% of tax payable, whichever is greater.
Late payment penalty: 5% of the unpaid tax plus 1% per month interest.

SECTION 6: INPUT VAT RECOVERY
-------------------------------
A VAT-registered person is entitled to deduct input VAT paid on goods and services
acquired for the purpose of making taxable supplies. Input VAT on exempt supplies is
not recoverable. Partial exemption rules apply where a person makes both taxable and
exempt supplies; the recoverable proportion is calculated using the standard method:

  Recoverable % = (Taxable Supplies / Total Supplies) × 100

SECTION 7: ELECTRONIC TAX INVOICE MANAGEMENT SYSTEM (eTIMS)
-------------------------------------------------------------
With effect from 1 January 2024, all VAT-registered traders must issue tax invoices
through the KRA Electronic Tax Invoice Management System (eTIMS). Non-compliance
renders the input VAT claim invalid for the purchaser.

KRA Compliance Requirement: All invoices must carry a KRA-assigned invoice number
and a QR code linking to the eTIMS portal verification.

SECTION 8: WITHHOLDING VAT
----------------------------
The following categories of purchasers are required to withhold VAT at source:
- Government ministries, departments, and agencies
- Public bodies and parastatals
- Listed companies and their subsidiaries

Withholding VAT rate: 2% of the taxable value of the supply.
The withheld amount must be remitted to KRA by the 20th of the following month.

SECTION 9: PENALTIES FOR VAT EVASION
--------------------------------------
Tax evasion relating to VAT carries the following penalties under the Tax Procedures Act:
- Civil penalty: 100% of the evaded tax
- Criminal prosecution: imprisonment for up to 3 years and/or a fine
- Interest: 1% per month on underpaid tax, compounded monthly
- Failure to maintain VAT records: KES 500,000 penalty

SECTION 10: RECORD KEEPING REQUIREMENTS
-----------------------------------------
VAT-registered persons must maintain the following records for a minimum of 5 years:
- All sales and purchase invoices (original)
- VAT account (monthly summary of output and input VAT)
- Cash receipts and payment records
- Import and export documentation
- Stock records and inventory sheets
- Bank statements reconciled to VAT returns

All records must be available for inspection by KRA officers at any time.
